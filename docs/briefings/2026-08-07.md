Two things happened around BL-826's QA pass worth naming precisely, because
they are two different classes of finding:

1. **A benign-outcome process incident.** While investigating BL-650's merge
   safety, QA found the master checkout dirty (a staged reversion of BL-835's
   fix) and independently "restored" it — discarding what was actually
   **BL-839**'s own deliberately-preserved drift-repro evidence — before a
   coordinator note explaining that landed. The outcome was harmless (`main`
   now correctly matches the working tree; BL-839's evidence already lives as
   static text in its own ticket YAML; its acceptance uses synthetic
   fixtures, not the live state), but the pattern — a review-stage role
   auto-repairing state it stumbles into instead of surfacing and asking — is
   exactly what BL-839 itself warns an automated detector must never do.
   Logged for pattern-recognition; no ticket needed, no data lost.
2. **A real, previously-undocumented mechanism gap.** QA's own harness
   explicitly forbids it from re-checking its mailbox while it holds
   in-process work ("do not run `ready_for_next.sh` again"). That means once
   QA is genuinely blocked mid-verification on something only a human can
   supply, a coordinator note sitting in its inbox is structurally
   unreachable until QA finishes or abandons the very parcel it's blocked
   on — a real deadlock, not a slow-poll annoyance. The coordinator worked
   around it this shift by calling `role_ask.bb` directly on QA's behalf
   (BL-773, "coordinator asks through the role-ask path," is the paused
   ticket that would formalize this — it just became visibly load-bearing).
   See *Flagged* and *Asks*.
