**The pack switch to `full-forge` is real throughput, at a real
fragility cost.** Split the lean ledger at the ~18:01 UTC switch and
compared: total queue-wait time across roles fell from 1668.8 min to
120.9 min, and total processing time fell from 653.5 min to 451.6 min
(small, confounded sample — not a controlled comparison, but a large
enough gap to be meaningful). The mechanism is obvious: mono-router's
single rotating resident makes every role wait its turn; full-forge
gives every role its own live session. But full-forge produced three
distinct incidents mono-router's single-resident design structurally
can't:

1. **`rotate_to_role.sh` stole the standing specifier pane and booted it
   as a second `coder`**, because its addressing logic ("first
   non-coordinator `roles.tsv` session") assumes a mono-router pack and
   full-forge has no such singleton resident. The stolen pane went
   unresponsive after a wedged API-error retry loop with real unsaved
   work in flight; recovered by force-rotating it back
   (`SWARMFORGE_ROTATE_FORCE=1 rotate_to_role.sh specifier`) after
   confirming the "unfinished parcel" refusal was itself the same
   collision bug, not real risk. Root-caused, ticketed, and **fixed and
   closed same shift as BL-931** — but BL-931's own fix broke BL-805's
   property-lane fixture (**BL-936**, pending approval) and there's a
   second related gap in bash-version portability (**BL-937**, pending
   approval).
2. **The coder mistakenly edited a full ticket's worth of files
   (BL-932) directly in the shared master checkout** instead of its own
   `.worktrees/coder` — its OS-level cwd was correct, but its Edit/Write
   tool calls used master-checkout absolute paths. Self-caught after a
   coordinator nudge, redone cleanly in the right worktree, shipped
   (`940a35c5c`) with no data lost — but it's a real near-miss on a
   shared-checkout architecture that has no structural guard against it.
3. **Real `.git/index.lock` contention in the shared master checkout,
   repeatedly**, between the coordinator's own bookkeeping and concurrent
   specifier/coder activity in the same physical directory. Every
   instance this shift self-resolved on retry with no data loss (the
   commit-integrity helper's rollback-on-failure held up correctly every
   time), but it happened enough times to be a pattern, not a fluke.

**Host load was genuinely oversubscribed under full-forge**: 16.6/14.9/
19.9 (1/5/15-min) on a 4-core host — 4-5x oversubscribed, sustained, with
two concurrent `vitest run` invocations observed at once. This matches
the standing "skip Stryker under `>>2x` cores" lesson and is a plausible
contributor to QA's processing-time increase (see above) and to at least
one property-test suite hitting real-process timeouts mid-verification.
Filed an intake, specifier minted **BL-935** (cap vitest to 1 fork under
a live full-forge pack on macOS, distinct from the existing memory-based
worker cap) — pending approval.
