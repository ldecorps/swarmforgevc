## What was built

Three pieces, split along the line invariant 1 draws:

| piece | file | role |
|---|---|---|
| the fold | `extension/src/metrics/ritualLedger.ts` | pure: classify by path area, count volume, compute subject dominance, pick candidates |
| the cadence | `extension/src/metrics/ritualLedgerProducer.ts` + `extension/src/tools/run-ritual-ledger-producer.ts` + a `handoffd.bb` sweep | MEASUREMENT accrues on the daemon's own tick, into `.swarmforge/telemetry/ritual-ledger.json` |
| the read | `extension/src/metrics/closingCeremonyRun.ts` | the ceremony READS the store and applies selection + suppression at assembly |

**The detector is the ticket's own, unchanged**: dominance — the share of a
class's commits carrying its single most common *normalized* subject.
Normalization collapses what a generator varies (ticket ids, dates, hashes,
bare numbers), so a scripted ritual's 484 commits read as one subject and an
agent's read as a long tail.

**Thresholds are two exported constants at the top of the file**, not buried in
a predicate, because the ticket says they will need tuning and that tuning
should be cheap: `RITUAL_VOLUME_FLOOR = 50`, `RITUAL_DOMINANCE_CEILING = 0.5`.
0.5 is a wide-margin choice against the ticket's own measured spread — the
scripted classes sit at 0.97 and 1.00, the hand-made one at 0.01, so nothing
real is anywhere near the line.

**Where the split actually falls, and why it is not arbitrary.** The ledger
stores raw per-class statistics only. Selection (thresholds) and suppression
(open tickets) happen at packet assembly, against the tickets open *now*. That
is what makes invariant 2 immediate — a ticket minted since the last sweep
silences its class on the very next packet, with no re-measurement — while
invariant 1 stays true, because nothing a ceremony does can alter what was
measured.

## Declared invariants (BL-654)

All three have a coder-authored property test in this parcel, each shown
non-vacuous against a deliberately broken implementation, each independent —
breaking one failed **only** its own property, with the other three still
green:

- **Invariant 1** (computed outside, merely read) — broke it by having the
  ceremony rewrite the ledger it had just read. `skipped ceremonies lose no
  measurement` failed; 2, 3 and the detector property passed. The property
  generates a random pattern of ran/skipped ceremonies and asserts a *later*
  ceremony still offers exactly what the store supports, and that no ceremony
  mutated the store. Floors asserted: at least one run that skipped a
  ceremony, at least one that reached a candidate, at least one below the
  volume floor.
- **Invariant 2** (a ticketed class is never offered again) — broke it by
  dropping the suppression term from the candidate filter. `a class an open
  ticket names is never offered` failed; the others passed. The suppressing
  text is **derived from the class under test** rather than drawn
  independently, so every generated pair is a suppression candidate by
  construction — drawing both sides freely would almost never collide and the
  property would pass while testing nothing. Floor asserted: at least one run
  reached a class that *would* have been offered without the ticket, so
  suppression demonstrably did work rather than riding on a below-floor class.
- **Invariant 3** (proposes, never mints) — broke it by writing a
  `backlog/active/AUTO-<class>.yaml` for each candidate. `a candidate is
  evidence, never an auto-minted ticket` failed; the others passed. It counts
  every file under `backlog/` before and after and asserts the ceremony
  recorded no outcome of its own when candidates exist.

A fourth, non-declared property pins the detector itself: a scripted class is
never offered and a hand-made class above the floor always is, with floors
asserting both shapes are generated.

Both broken files were restored and confirmed byte-identical by `diff`, and no
`BREAK` marker survives anywhere under `extension/src/`.

## An observation on BL-1364, already forwarded

BL-1364's step handler (`bl1364MechanicalShareReadableSteps.js:56`) allocates
its fixture root with `mkTmpDir`. Under Vitest that root is swept by a
registered `afterEach`; the acceptance runner has no such hook, so under
`run_acceptance.sh` the root simply leaks. This handler uses
`mkProcessTmpDir` instead — removal registered on process exit, which needs no
hook and does not reach for the prefix-glob sweep that would delete a
concurrent run's fixtures (BL-1390).

Minor, and not this parcel's to fix: BL-1364 is with the cleaner. Recorded so
whoever holds it can decide, rather than discovered twice.
